Replaces the undifferentiated Content block. One block, several variants, with a restricted rich-text field so every page looks consistent.

Example content is realistic but placeholder (Danfoss Turbocor TTH/TGH). Specs and claims are not verified. Bracketed ‘Image’ boxes stand for photography.

Variants

  • Text + image (image left) — First block of a run.
  • Text + image (image right) — Auto-flips when it follows another Text + image block.
  • Auto-alternating run of three — Left → right → left, as it appears on a page.
  • Text only
  • Feature highlight
  • Callout / note

Responsive behaviour

MobileImage always above text
TabletSide by side 50/50
Desktop / Wide5/7 split; text capped at ~72 characters per line

Editor / design guidance

  • Consecutive Text + image blocks alternate sides automatically: image left, then right, then left. Editors do not choose.
  • Good for feature runs (oil-free → variable-speed efficiency → quiet running).
  • No free headings or inline styles: this is what stops pages looking different.

Source of truth: docs/block-library.md, docs/design-logic.md. R = required, O = optional.

How it works

Oil-free compression

Magnetic levitation, no oil

The compressor shaft floats on digitally controlled magnetic bearings, so there is no metal-to-metal contact and no lubrication system to maintain.

  • Digital control adjusts bearing position thousands of times a second
  • Variable speed gives high part-load efficiency
  • Fewer moving parts means lower lifetime cost

Quiet running

  • Low vibration eases structural and isolation requirements
  • Lower sound levels help with planning noise limits

Why oil-free matters

Cleaner, more efficient heat transfer

Oil in a refrigerant circuit coats heat-exchanger surfaces and reduces efficiency over time. Removing it keeps performance at design level for the life of the machine.

For sites with strict hygiene or reliability requirements, an oil-free circuit also removes a common failure mode.
